# 4. Content

## Opening — exactly one paragraph

Build the opening around four ideas within one natural paragraph:

1. Who Junwen is: Start with "I am a recent master's graduate with ..." Complete the sentence with his most relevant hands-on experience for the role. For example: "I am a recent master's graduate with practical experience building AI-powered solutions for real-world problems using LLMs and machine learning." Use only experience supported by the CV, verified personal context, or verified project resources. Name the degree subject only when it matters to the role or the Additional Instructions ask for it.
2. Why the role interests him and what he can bring: Connect his genuine interest to the chance to apply his existing hands-on experience, and explain how that experience could help him contribute effectively. Show this connection through evidence rather than saying "I am a strong fit".
3. Long-term alignment: Explain how the role fits Junwen's long-term career goal and would help him continue growing towards it.
4. What he is looking for now: Explain that Junwen wants to apply his skills and experience to real business challenges in a professional engineering team, learn from experienced engineers, build greater engineering maturity, and make a practical impact.

Treat the opening as an executive summary, not an evidence section. Keep it between 55 and 75 words and focus only on Junwen's overall profile, high-level interest in the opportunity, relevant capability direction, and career alignment. Move all supporting evidence and project-specific explanation to the project-experience section.

If removing a detail does not change the reader's understanding of who Junwen is, why he is interested, and what general value he offers, remove it from the opening.

Adjust the emphasis based on the decision made in Before Writing:

- For an immediate-contribution role, give more space to what Junwen can bring, his relevant hands-on experience, and how he can contribute. Keep the growth-focused points brief.
- For a growth-focused role, give more space to his motivation, long-term goal, and desire to grow in a professional engineering environment. Make clear that he already has relevant hands-on experience and is now looking for the right environment to apply and develop it.

# 6. Reference and Style

- Write in Junwen's first-person voice using natural, professional New Zealand English.
- Use a reference cover letter for both reusable content and its voice, tone, sentence flow, and level of detail.
- If it is for a similar role, consider its project choices, technical focus, explanation of relevance, motivation, and contribution.
- Do not use the reference as a separate source of facts. Reuse a factual claim only when the current CV, verified personal context, or verified project resources support it.
- Do not carry over the old role, company, or outdated application details.
- The current facts and writing rules always come before the reference.
- Reuse useful ideas and framing, but rewrite them for the current job description. Do not copy whole passages without adapting them.
- Unless the Additional Instructions say otherwise, avoid generic praise, repetition, exaggerated claims, and sentences that simply repeat the job description.
