# 4. Content

## Opening — exactly one paragraph

Build the opening around two main points within one natural paragraph:

1. Who Junwen is and what he brings: In the first sentence, start with "I am a recent master's graduate with ..." and complete the sentence with his most relevant hands-on experience for the role. Use the current CV as the primary source for that experience and supplement it, when useful, with verified personal context or verified project resources. Describe the experience at a high level. Do not include detailed technologies, implementation steps, metrics, or project explanation here, because the project-experience section will provide the supporting detail. Name the degree subject only when it matters to the role or the Additional Instructions ask for it.
   - For a Computer Vision / Applied Machine Learning role, position Junwen as a recent master's graduate with hands-on experience applying computer vision to a real laboratory problem.
   - For an Applied AI / AI Automation role, position Junwen as a recent master's graduate with practical experience building AI-powered solutions that reduce manual work.
2. Why the role interests him: Use the remaining one or two sentences to explain two connected reasons:
   - Junwen already has hands-on experience in the area on which the role focuses, so the opportunity would allow him to apply his existing skills and experience to real business challenges in a professional engineering environment, continue growing, and make a practical impact.
   - The role aligns with his long-term goal of becoming a well-rounded AI solution builder who can take real-world problems from problem understanding and solution design through implementation, evaluation, and delivery, making it a valuable next step towards that goal.

Express these reasons specifically in relation to the role rather than as generic career statements. For example: "I am particularly excited about this opportunity because it closely aligns with my agricultural computer-vision experience, while offering the chance to apply my skills in a professional environment and make a practical impact. It would also be a valuable next step towards my long-term goal of becoming a well-rounded AI solution builder."

Treat the opening as an executive summary, not an evidence section. Keep it as concise as possible, using as few words as possible while naturally covering Junwen's overall profile, high-level interest in the opportunity, relevant capability direction, and career alignment. Move all supporting evidence and project-specific explanation to the project-experience section.

Keep both reasons under the second point, but adjust their relative emphasis based on the decision made in Before Writing:

- For an immediate-contribution role, give more of the opening to Junwen's relevant hands-on experience and explain at a high level how that foundation would help him apply his skills to real business challenges and contribute effectively. Mention alignment with his long-term goal briefly, normally in one concise clause or sentence. Do not add the detailed project evidence reserved for the project-experience section.
- For a growth-focused role, give more of the opening to why the role's work and professional engineering environment align with Junwen's long-term career direction. Explain how working on real business challenges, learning from experienced engineers, and developing greater engineering maturity would help him progress towards becoming a well-rounded AI solution builder. Still make clear that he already has relevant hands-on experience to apply and develop; do not present him as starting from scratch or interested only in what he can learn.

### First project paragraph — project purpose, outcome, and relevant work

- Begin with a natural transition explaining that Junwen's relevant practical experience comes from hands-on project work, then introduce the selected project and explain what he built, what problem or workflow it was intended to address, and its practical result. Describe it as a personal, independent, or laboratory project only when that context is supported by the trusted sources. Do not begin the paragraph abruptly with "I built ...". A suitable pattern is: "I have gained practical experience in [relevant area] through hands-on project work. For example, I built [project] to [purpose or outcome]." Adapt the transition naturally to the role rather than copying this pattern mechanically.
- Explain what Junwen personally did in the project. After determining which project to present, identify the job description's core requirements and map them to evidence about that project in Junwen's current CV and verified project resources. Prioritise only the evidence that most directly demonstrates those requirements; do not try to cover every project detail. Represent Junwen's own work and outcomes accurately.
- For a role focused on using AI to streamline a manual or repetitive workflow, highlight Junwen's experience identifying where AI could help, designing and implementing an AI-supported solution, evaluating its performance, and turning it into a usable product or working prototype. Apply this framing to either StackRadar or Automated Nematode Egg Detection, depending on which project was selected for the role.

### Second project paragraph — learning and relevance to the role

Build the second project paragraph around two connected points:

1. What Junwen learned and gained from the project:
   - Begin the paragraph by summarising at a high level the practical capability or engineering experience he gained. Ground the summary in the project evidence from the first paragraph.
   - Do not present the learning as a list of programming languages, frameworks, tools, or isolated technical skills. Summarise a coherent end-to-end capability or engineering process instead.
   - Before finalising only this opening learning-and-gain portion of the paragraph, silently draft and compare two alternatives:
     1. Evidence-driven alternative: Follow the rules above and let the selected project evidence and the job description's core requirements determine the strongest high-level capability to emphasise.
     2. Direction-based alternative: Use the fixed emphasis for the role's classified direction, while grounding every claim in the current CV and verified project resources:

        - For an Applied AI / AI Automation role, emphasise Junwen's hands-on experience identifying a repetitive or time-consuming manual workflow, determining where AI could help, designing and implementing an AI-supported solution, evaluating its effectiveness, and turning it into a usable product or workflow.
   - Select the alternative that is better supported by Junwen's evidence, aligns more closely with the job description's core requirements, and makes his relevant capability clearer and more compelling to a recruiter or hiring manager. Use only the selected alternative to open this paragraph. Do not mention the alternatives or comparison, and do not apply this comparison to the rest of the paragraph or any other cover-letter section.

2. What that experience means for the role and team:
   - Use the selected learning-and-gain summary as the foundation, then explain naturally how it relates to the work, contribution, and development expected in the role and team. Do not repeat the opening's high-level motivation.
   - Do not use blunt statements such as "these are key requirements for this internship". Show relevance through the connection between the broader capability and the role.
   - For an immediate-contribution role, explain how the experience or engineering capability would help Junwen address the role's requirements, get up to speed quickly, and contribute effectively to the team.
   - For a growth-focused role, explain how Junwen is looking to apply and develop this existing foundation through real business challenges in a professional engineering environment, continue learning from experienced engineers or developers, and grow into a stronger contributor to the team.
